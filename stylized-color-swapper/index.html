Stylized Color Swapper & Material Optimizer

Complete Technical Reference & Editor Guide

The Stylized Color Swapper & Material Optimizer is a monolithic Editor utility engineered to eliminate the traditional "Photoshop to Unity" asset variation bottleneck. By allowing artists to interactively scan, paint, and re-bake stylized assets directly inside a custom 3D viewport, it empowers rapid creation of infinite prop variations with zero texture memory overhead.

Zero-Overhead Philosophy: Variations generated via the Vertex Bake & Pack mode cost absolutely no additional texture memory and utilize the optimized VCCore material for single-pass instanced rendering across thousands of variants.

Core Architecture & Workflow

The tool operates entirely non-destructively on your scene models. The typical lifecycle of a variation is as follows:

  1. Selection: Drag a target GameObject or Prefab into the tool's Target Object field.
  2. Scanning: The tool evaluates your mesh against its original texture or vertex stream, extracting a unified Swatch Palette based on exact UV mappings and material zones.
  3. Painting: Using the interactive 3D viewport, you paint new base colors, author directional 3D gradients, assign procedural PBR material IDs, adjust surface normal smoothing, paint emissive glow, and paint natural vertex wind sway weights directly onto the 3D surface.
  4. Generation: The tool bakes all painted data into a new isolated Mesh asset, packs PBR data into secondary UV channels, and saves a Phantom Prefab ready for level placement.

Scanning & Recolor Modes

The tool offers specific modes for scanning meshes and outputting the final recolored data.

Scan Modes

Mode Behavior & Use Case
Texture Palette (Default) Scans the base map of the material and groups mesh triangles by their exact sampled color. Ideal for traditional flat-shaded or gradient-mapped stylized models (e.g., Synty, Polygon packs).
Vertex Colors Reads pre-existing vertex colors on the mesh instead of the texture, averaging all 3 vertices of each triangle for a stable sample. Use this if the model is already vertex-painted from software like Blender or ZBrush, or when reloading a saved DPT prefab to continue editing.
Material Colors Reads the solid color directly off each assigned unlit material instead of a texture or vertex data. Use this for objects whose materials have no textures or vertex colors and are just flat colors set via the material's color wheel.

Two additional toggles refine scanning behavior: Combine Similar Colors (Alt+C) merges near-identical sampled colors into a single swatch at scan time, and Split Disconnected Islands (Alt+I) separates physically disconnected clusters sharing the same color into independently paintable swatches. When saving, identical swatches automatically fold into single master pixels in the output texture atlas.

Scan Fidelity & Geometry Options

A few additional settings control how faithfully the tool reads and rebuilds your source geometry:

  • Auto-Uncompress Textures (Scan Mode): Temporarily marks compressed textures (DXT5, BC7, etc.) as uncompressed while scanning, avoiding block artifacts and color banding that would otherwise degrade palette accuracy and cause edge seams. If the tool detects compressed textures on your target, it shows a warning with a Re-Scan (Lossless) button to force a one-off high-fidelity rescan.
  • Adaptive Tessellation: Subdivides large triangles automatically to improve vertex-painting resolution, particularly for preserving fine texture detail (text, decals, small logos) when baking into vertex colors. Controls: Protect Smooth Surfaces (prevents slicing on curved geometry like wheels, avoiding shading artifacts), Max Subdivision (1–5, the depth limit for adaptive splitting), and Color Threshold (how much color variation across a triangle is needed to trigger a split). Effectiveness varies by mesh: it captures fine detail well on hard-surface models but has little to no visible effect on already-low-poly, largely uniform-colored meshes.
  • Auto-Unwrap (Lossless): Dynamically generates a mathematically clean UV map during bake, specifically for Feature ID detail mapping. Fixes models with poor or missing original UVs without modifying the source asset. When enabled, a Hard Edge Cutoff angle slider controls where the unwrapper is willing to cut a seam to relieve UV pressure — lower values hide seams on sharper corners.
  • Smooth Normals (Per-Swatch Geometry Smoothing): Recalculates and averages vertex normals across coincident split edges using a continuous angle-thresholding algorithm. Available in both Vertex Bake & Pack and Texture Coloring modes. Allows selective rounding of faceted, low-poly geometry (such as campfire stones, chopped logs, or terrain) without altering the underlying mesh vertex count.

Recolor Modes (Output)

Mode Output Format
Texture Coloring Traditional approach. Recolor the swatches and export a brand new PNG texture atlas. Used for legacy pipelines or mobile targets with strict vertex limitations. Supports Normal Smoothing and Texel Density scaling.
Vertex Bake & Pack (Recommended) Injects colors directly into the mesh Vertex Colors (COLOR). Encodes advanced PBR values (Metallic, Smoothness, AO) and procedural Feature IDs into unused UV channels (TEXCOORD1, TEXCOORD2). Bypasses textures entirely while unlocking Directional 3D Gradients, Live Wind Painting, Normal Smoothing, and Zones.

Multi-Scope Selection Engine

The tool includes a robust three-tier geometric selection hierarchy paired with dynamic polygon extraction. Switch between selection scopes by clicking their buttons in the toolbar or pressing I:

Face Selection

Target individual triangles/polygons with pinpoint precision. Ideal for painting fine accents, small trim lines, glowing runes, or specific facets without affecting adjoining faces.

UV Island Selection

Selects the entire contiguous UV cluster under your cursor. Perfect for recoloring distinct texture regions that were unwrapped together as a single continuous UV piece.

3D Mesh Shell Selection

Selects the entire physically encapsulated 3D geometry element across all UV seams. Instantly selects detached accessories, complete hair clumps, armor plates, weapons, and clothing layers in a single click.

Dynamic Face Extract (Q)

Dynamically isolates and carves out single polygons on-the-fly without needing to split the entire mesh upfront. Extracted faces become independent swatches ready for instant recoloring or gradient application.

Directional 3D Gradients & Vertex Wind Sway

Exclusive to Vertex Bake & Pack mode, the Gradient Paint (G) tool allows you to author directional 3D gradients across any material channel (Color, Metallic, Smoothness, AO, Emissive, and Wind) by clicking and dragging directly across the model in the 3D viewport.

Directional 3D Gradients

Click to set the start point (anchor) and drag to define the direction and length of the gradient. Gradients are scale-invariant and calculate accurately on any object size.

  • Blend Modes: Choose between Multiply, Replace, or SoftLight.
  • Alpha: Adjusts overall gradient intensity and opacity.
  • Sharpness: Controls the falloff curve from soft linear transitions to crisp high-contrast thresholds.
  • Clear: The dedicated Clear button immediately strips active gradients from the current selection.

Gradient-Based Vertex Wind Painting

Switch the view channel to Wind and drag a gradient from the plant/prop base to its tips to paint natural, progressive sway weight. Stems stay rooted firmly at 0.0 while leaves and branches sway dynamically at 1.0.

  • Simulate Wind Preview: Toggle the Simulate Wind button in the Wind channel toolbar to preview live real-time GPU vertex wind animation directly inside the viewport before saving.
  • Zero Performance Cost: Wind weights are baked straight into the vertex stream and evaluated at runtime by the lightweight VCCore shader.

Understanding Vertex Colors: Benefits & Limits

Vertex Bake & Pack is the recommended mode, but it's a genuinely different way of storing color and material data than a texture, with its own trade-offs. Understanding these will save you time diagnosing results that look "off."

Benefit: Zero Texture Memory

Every variant reuses the same tiny VCCore material and shares GPU-instanced draw calls (see the Zero-Overhead note above) — no per-variant texture memory cost, even across thousands of prop variants.

Limit: Resolution Is Tied to Geometry

A texture can hold fine detail (text, decals, logos) regardless of mesh density. Vertex color resolution is capped by how many vertices exist — color only changes where a vertex exists to carry it. Fine detail gets smoothed or lost unless you add geometry, which is what Adaptive Tessellation (above) is for.

Limit: Zones & Emissive Need Texture Mode

In this tool, the Zones and Emissive paint channels are only available in Vertex Bake & Pack mode. If you need Zones or Emissive on a texture-based setup instead, don't use this tool's vertex output for it — assign your VCCore material in its non-vertex-color mode, which exposes dedicated texture fields for both.

Limit: Gradient Paint Is Vertex-Only

Gradient Paint isn't available in Texture Coloring mode. With Optimize Palette Size on, every swatch's UVs get squashed down to a single pixel, leaving no room to represent a directional gradient at all. With it off, texture UV layouts aren't consistent enough between models to reliably map a gradient onto them.

Ambient Occlusion in Vertex Coloring mode: Textured AO maps don't conform reliably onto vertex data — a texture's AO detail is denser than the mesh's vertices can represent, and errors compound. Because of this, when you're in Vertex Coloring mode using a Texture-based scan, every swatch starts with AO reset to a flat 1.0 (no occlusion) rather than inheriting the source texture's AO. From there you can manually paint AO values back in, or run the Raycast AO Baker to compute genuine occlusion directly against your geometry.
Painted AO affects shadowing under Stylized Lighting: If your target material has Stylized Lighting enabled, any AO value you paint or bake isn't purely a decorative darkening — it factors into the shadow response itself. Overly dark painted AO can read as unwanted shadowing rather than subtle occlusion under that lighting mode.

The Interactive 3D Viewport

The tool bypasses the standard Unity Scene View in favor of a specialized dual-rendering sandbox. This allows you to paint props without dragging them into your game scene.

Before / After Preview

The embedded viewport shows your live "After" recolor by default. Hold O to temporarily peek at the original, unmodified "Before" mesh — release to return to the live recolor.

Scene View Comparison

Press B to spawn temporary preview proxies directly in your main Unity Scene view, complete with real-time lighting evaluation. (Will automatically clean up on window close).

Auto-Rotation

Press T (or Space, when no text field has focus) to enable a slow, continuous turntable rotation for reviewing your lighting response from all angles.

Dynamic Lighting

Fully supports dynamic Editor preview lighting, ensuring your metallic and smoothness values accurately reflect environment probes and directional highlights.

Show/Hide Preview

Press V to toggle visibility of the embedded viewport entirely, freeing up window space for the swatch list.

Camera Sync

The Sync Scene and Sync Game toggle buttons above the viewport lock the Scene View camera and/or Main Camera to match the tool's preview camera — useful for lining up a Game View recording with what you're painting.

Marquee Select

Shift + click-drag in the embedded viewport (while not in Paint Mode or Pick Color mode) draws a selection box and selects every swatch whose faces fall inside it — a fast way to multi-select swatches without Ctrl-clicking each one in the list.

Live Wind Motion Preview

Toggle Simulate Wind inside the Wind view channel to watch foliage, grass, and props sway in real-time right inside the editor viewport.

Texel Density & Normal Smoothing Controls

Located directly within the Selected Area Recolor panel, these specialized tools ensure uniform procedural detail scaling and advanced geometry curvature control across your meshes:

Texel Density Tools

Allows you to target a uniform pixel density (px/m) based on your intended Atlas Size. The joined Calculate & Assign Scale and Auto-Normalize Scale buttons evaluate the 3D surface area against UV area across all zones, ensuring procedural noise (wood grain, cloth weaves) tiles evenly without stretching across different sub-meshes.

Smooth Normals & Angle Threshold

Toggling Smooth Normals on any selected swatch unlocks an angle slider (0°–180°) situated directly on the same row. It operates selectively on each swatch:

  • 0° - Completely preserves original/flat faceted imported normals.
  • 10°–60° - Progressively smooths shallow creases while keeping sharp corners hard.
  • 120°–180° - Fully rounds out steep polygon angles into smooth curved surfaces.
Scale-Invariant Geometry Engine: The normal smoothing and gradient painting systems utilize bounds-normalized spatial hashing and unit vector cross-products. Whether a model is a 100m dragon, a 1m dog, or a 1cm campfire imported with a 0.01 scale factor, normals and gradients calculate accurately with zero underflow or distortion.

Advanced PBR & Feature ID Assignments

When operating in Vertex Bake & Pack mode, the tool allows you to assign procedural shading characteristics directly to specific geometric faces via "Feature IDs".

Select a swatch in the list view, and use the Feature ID dropdown to assign specialized material types:

  • Default (None) - Standard PBR response based on your slider settings, no procedural detail.
  • Wood - Applies a procedural base color tint in addition to a wood grain normal detail — the one Feature ID that affects base color as well as surface normal.
  • Bark, Steel, Copper, CastIron, Leather, Cloth, Fur, Stone, Rope, Foliage - Each applies its own procedural surface normal or clump detail (tri-planar mapped across the mesh).

Additional Sub-Mesh Controls

When a specialized Feature ID is assigned, additional controls unlock in the swatch editor:

  • Detail Scale: Adjusts the tiling density of the procedural noise (e.g., making wood grain tighter or metal scratches larger).
  • Detail Rotation: A 0–360° slider that rotates the procedural detail texture on this swatch — use it to fix directional textures (like Wood Grain) when the underlying UV mapping runs the wrong way.
  • Blend Detail: Blends the procedural detail across faces and UV seams using tri-planar projection averaging. When enabled, two independent sliders appear: Face controls how much detail blends across adjoining faces, and Seam controls blend strength specifically across UV seams.
  • Apply Wind: Enables flat wind sway on this swatch's detail with a Strength slider (or use Gradient Paint in the Wind channel for continuous directional sway).
Feature IDs require the _DETAIL keyword: If your Target Material doesn't have the _DETAIL shader keyword enabled, assigning a non-Default Feature ID shows a warning with an "Enable Procedural Details" button in the swatch editor. Click it to enable the keyword on the material directly (tiling size for the detail system is then adjustable on the material itself).

Zone Painting

In Vertex Bake & Pack mode, the Zones option in the Change Channel dropdown lets you paint a Zone ID (0–7, where 7 is Default) directly onto swatches. This is a separate data channel from Color/Metallic/etc. and is packed alongside the other PBR data at bake time.

Zone IDs are read at runtime by DPT_ZoneVariation to drive per-instance color variation — see the Zone Variation & Palettes page for how zones are consumed by the shader system. This section covers only the paint-time assignment inside the Recolor Tool itself:

  • Switch the view/paint channel to Zones. Swatches display in a distinct zone-index color rather than their actual paint color.
  • With a swatch selected, either drag the Zone slider (0–7) in the swatch editor, or paint directly in the viewport the same way you'd paint a color — the active zone value takes the place of the brush color while this channel is active.
  • Zone 7 is treated as the Default zone; use 0–6 for zones you intend to reference from gameplay code or shader variation logic.
Working with textures instead of vertex colors? This paint workflow only exists in Vertex Bake & Pack mode. For a texture-based prop that still needs Zones, set VCCore to its non-vertex-color mode instead, which exposes a dedicated texture field for zone data.

Palette Management

The tool includes a dual-palette workflow for maintaining artistic consistency across multiple assets.

  • Custom User Palette (Left): A grid of 18 persistent color slots. These colors are saved to your Editor Preferences and persist across all unity sessions. Click any slot to load it into your brush.
  • Imported Source Palette (Right): When you scan a mesh, its extracted colors are populated here.
  • Copy < Button: Clicking the arrow between the palettes copies the extracted colors from the current prop (Imported Palette) directly into your global Custom Palette for use on other assets.

You can also use the Export Palette (PNG) and Import Palette (PNG) buttons to save your custom swatches to disk as a 256x1 pixel texture strip, allowing you to share color schemes across team members or backup project themes.

Multi-Material PBR Overrides

When your Target Object has more than one material, each material that supplies textures gets its own collapsible group in the PBR input area, labeled with the source material name(s). Inside each group you can independently override that material's Base Color, and its Metallic/Smoothness/AO input — either as a single Packed MRAO Map (with a channel mapping selector) or as separate Metal, Smooth, and AO texture slots, depending on the material's PBR Mode. Leave a slot empty to fall back to whatever the tool auto-detected from that material.

Raycast AO Baker

A separate foldout from the main paint workflow, the Raycast AO Baker computes a fresh global ambient occlusion pass for your target by casting rays from every vertex, independent of whatever AO the source mesh or material already had baked in.

  • Rays Per Vertex: Sample count per vertex (16–512). Higher is cleaner but slower to compute.
  • Max Distance: How far a ray can travel before it's considered unoccluded.
  • Intensity: Overall strength multiplier for the computed occlusion.
  • Blur Passes: Smooths the raw raycast result to reduce noise.
  • Bake Target: Choose whether the result is written into the mesh's vertex AO data, or baked out to a standalone AO texture map (with a resolution field and a Match Base Color button to copy the current palette texture's resolution).

Click Compute & Bake Global Raycast AO to run it. This section is hidden when Texture Coloring mode has Optimize Palette Size enabled: baking AO to its own texture map needs each face to have independent, non-overlapping UVs, and Optimize Palette Size intentionally shrinks and overlaps UVs to save disk space — the two goals are incompatible, so the baker is hidden rather than producing broken results.

Phantom Asset Generation

Once you are satisfied with your 3D painting, use the buttons at the bottom of the window to save your work. Which ones are available depends on what you're working on:

  • Apply to Scene Object: Only shown when your Target Object is an instance already in the scene. Generates the new assets and saves them as a Prefab on disk, creates a backup of your original model, and seamlessly replaces the scene object with the new Prefab instance. The tool will automatically target this new instance so you can continue making adjustments.
  • Create Prefab: Saves a new, standalone prefab variant to your configured Save Path and leaves your current session pointed at the original target.

A Material Mode setting controls how the material asset itself is handled on save:

  • Create Variant of Target Material: Creates a new material variant per save, based on your configured Target Material.
  • Create Single Shared Variant (Merge All): All variants generated in a session share one material variant instead of getting their own.
  • Assign Shared Target Material (No New .mat): Points every output straight at the Target Material with no new material asset created at all.
  • Modify Existing Material In-Place: Edits the object's current material asset directly instead of creating anything new.
  • Keep Existing Material As-Is: Leaves the material assignment untouched entirely.

Whichever button you use, the save process performs the following:

  1. Clones the original mesh geometry.
  2. Injects your painted Color data into the mesh's COLOR channel.
  3. Packs your Metallic, Smoothness, and AO data efficiently into the mesh's TEXCOORD1 or TEXCOORD2 channels.
  4. Calculates angle-thresholded normal smoothing on designated swatches, recalculating tangent space.
  5. Saves the new mesh (and any generated textures, in Texture Coloring mode) under your configured Save Path.
  6. Generates or updates a Prefab that references the new mesh and assigns the appropriate VCCore material.
Audit System: The Footprint panel (and the F hotkey) let you preview the memory footprint of your in-progress asset before you save, so you can check the savings from bypassing traditional texture generation ahead of time rather than only finding out after export.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

I have toggled on Split Disconnected Islands but it still won't let me paint individual color islands.
Make sure you have toggled off Group Similar (CTRL + G) in the 3D Viewport section.
How do I paint directional wind sway on vegetation or cloth?
Switch the view/paint channel to Wind and enable Gradient Paint [G]. Click at the base of the plant/stem and drag towards the tip. Toggle Simulate Wind in the top toolbar to watch the model sway live in the viewport.
What is the difference between Face, Island, and Shell selection?
Face targets single triangles for surgical precision. Island selects contiguous UV clusters. Shell selects entire 3D detached geometry pieces (e.g. hair clumps, armor plates, weapons) across all UV seams. Cycle through them using the I hotkey.
I have Optimize Palette Size on with Split Disconnected Islands On and it isn't highlighting what I'm clicking on but I can still change its color.
You can fix this by creating a prefab and replacing it in the target object field, but it is caused by mirrored UVs on the original object.
Why aren't my low-poly rocks or logs smoothing when I turn on Smooth Normals?
Low-poly props have steep dihedral angles between adjacent faces (often 60° to 90°+). Increase the Smooth Normals slider towards 120°–180° to allow those large angles to blend together. A setting of 0° keeps faces completely flat.
Can I smooth only specific materials on my model without making everything round?
Yes! Smooth Normals is assigned on a per-swatch basis. You can enable smoothing on organic elements (wood, rocks, creature bodies) while leaving hard-surface elements (metal blades, armor plates) toggled off with crisp flat normals.
I clicked the model in Paint Mode but nothing happened.
Check that Eraser Mode [E] is not accidentally toggled on. The active painting brush is tied directly to your current View selection, so ensure you have the correct View enabled for the property you are trying to edit (e.g., you cannot paint Metallic values while in the Color view).
I can't see the "Detail Scale" or "Feature ID" settings on my swatches.
These advanced PBR controls only appear if your Recolor Mode is set to Vertex Bake & Pack. If you are in Texture Coloring mode, the tool assumes you are exporting a standard PNG and hides the GPU-specific parametric controls.
Ctrl+Z undid something else in my scene instead of my brush stroke.
Undo runs through Unity's native, global Undo history rather than a separate tool-only stack, so it's chronological across everything you've done in the Editor — if you touched something else in the scene after your last paint stroke, that's what undoes first. Keep undoing and your paint history will come up next in order.
My generated prefab is pink (Missing Material).
This occurs if your project is missing the targeted base materials (like VCCore). Ensure that the DPT core shader packages are fully imported and that your Unity Render Pipeline settings match the material targets.
The UI is lagging heavily when I drag to paint a high-poly mesh.
The raycasting system performs precise mathematical intersection against every triangle to ensure pixel-perfect swatch detection. If you are painting a model with 50,000+ polygons, increasing the combine similar tolerance can help to reduce the number of swatches.
