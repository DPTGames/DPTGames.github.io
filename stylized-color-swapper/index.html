Stylized Color Swapper & Material Optimizer

Technical Guide

The Color Swapper is a tool that lives inside Unity's editor and lets you change the colors and look of your 3D models without leaving Unity or opening an image-editing program. You pick a model, paint new colors onto it in a 3D preview window, and save the result as a ready-to-use version, all in a few clicks, with no separate image files to manage.

Runs efficiently: When you save using Vertex mode, your color variations don't need any extra image files, and Unity can display thousands of differently-colored copies of the same model at once without slowing down. With Texture mode, you can make entire scenes run off 1 material with 1 micro-atlas.

How It Works, Step by Step

The tool never changes your original files by accident, it always works on a copy, and nothing is saved until you click a save button. Here's the general process:

  1. Pick a model: Drag the object or model you want to recolor into the tool's window.
  2. Scan it: The tool looks at the model's current colors and sorts its surface into a list of color groups (called "swatches") that you can edit one at a time, or several at once.
  3. Paint it: In the 3D preview window, you paint new colors, blend smooth color transitions, apply realistic material looks (like wood or metal), round off sharp edges, add a glow, or set up gentle wind movement, all by clicking and dragging directly on the model.
  4. Save it: Once you're happy with it, the tool saves your changes as a new, ready-to-drop-into-your-game version of the model.

Scanning Your Model & Choosing How to Save

Before you can paint, the tool needs to read your model's current colors. There are a few ways it can do that, and a couple of ways it can save the result.

How the Tool Reads Your Model's Colors

Option What It Does & When to Use It
From the Model's Image (Default) Looks at the color image already applied to the model and groups its surface by matching colors. This is the right choice for most stylized models (like Synty or Polygon-style packs).
From Colors Already Painted on the Model Reads colors that are already stored directly on the model's surface rather than in an image. Use this if the model was made in something like Blender or ZBrush, or if you're reopening a model you already saved with this tool and want to keep editing it.
From a Plain Flat Color For a model that has no image and no painted colors, just a single flat color chosen from a color wheel. Use this mode to read that flat color instead.

Two extra options fine-tune how scanning works: Combine Similar Colors (Alt+C) merges colors that are nearly identical into a single group, so you're not stuck editing near-duplicates separately. Split Disconnected Islands (Alt+I) keeps physically separate pieces that happen to share the same color (like two arms) as independently editable groups, instead of lumping them together. When you save, any groups that end up with identical colors are automatically merged back together to keep the result efficient.

Extra Accuracy Settings

A few more settings control how carefully the tool reads and rebuilds your model:

  • Read Compressed Images at Full Quality: Some image files are compressed to save space, which can make colors look slightly blurry or blocky when sampled directly. This option temporarily undoes that compression while scanning, so the colors it picks up are accurate. If it spots a compressed image, it will offer a one-click button to redo the scan at full quality.
  • Add Detail Automatically: If a model needs extra fine detail preserved (like small text, a logo, or a decal) when its colors are saved directly onto the model rather than an image, this setting automatically adds a bit of extra geometry only where it's needed to capture that detail. You can protect naturally curved surfaces (like wheels) from being cut up unnecessarily, limit how much extra detail is added, and set how big a color difference has to be before extra detail kicks in. It works best on blocky, hard-surface models, it won't do much for models that are already low-detail and mostly one color.
  • Fix Missing Wrap Layout: Some models don't have a clean "layout" for how a flat image wraps around their 3D shape. This automatically builds a clean one when needed, without touching your original file. A slider controls how aggressively it's allowed to add seams (the invisible cut lines needed to unfold a 3D shape flat) a lower value hides those seams on sharper corners.
  • Round Off Sharp Edges (Per Piece): Smooths out how light reflects off blocky, low-detail surfaces (like campfire stones or chopped logs) without adding any extra geometry, it just changes how the lighting looks. Works no matter which save option you use.

How the Result Gets Saved

Option What You Get
Save as an Image The traditional approach, recolors your model and creates a brand-new image file, just like the original had. Best for older projects or mobile games that need to keep things lightweight.
Save Directly on the Model (Recommended) Stores your colors and material settings (metal, shine, shadowing) directly on the model itself instead of in a separate image. This skips images entirely and unlocks extra features like smooth color gradients, live wind animation, edge smoothing, and zones (covered further below).

Ways to Select Parts of Your Model

The tool gives you three ways to select what you want to paint, plus a quick shortcut. Switch between them using the toolbar buttons or by pressing I:

Single Surface

Selects one small triangle at a time, for pinpoint precision. Great for tiny details like a trim line or a single glowing rune.

Connected Region

Selects a whole connected patch of the model that shares one continuous area on its image. Handy for recoloring a clearly defined region in one click.

Whole Piece

Selects an entire physically separate part of the model, like a whole armor plate, a sword, or a clump of hair, in a single click.

Quick Pull-Out (Q)

Instantly splits off just the surface under your cursor into its own separately-editable piece, without needing to split the whole model first.

Smooth Color Gradients & Gentle Wind Sway

Only available when saving directly on the model (the recommended option). The Gradient tool (G) lets you click and drag across the model to smoothly blend from one color or setting to another, instead of applying one flat value everywhere.

Smooth Gradients

Click to set a starting point, then drag to set the direction and length of the blend. This works accurately no matter how big or small the model is.

  • Blend Style: Choose how the gradient combines with what's already there, darken it, fully replace it, or blend it in more subtly.
  • Strength: How strong or faint the gradient looks overall.
  • Sharpness: How gradual or abrupt the transition is.
  • Clear: Instantly removes any gradient from what you've currently selected.

Painting Wind Movement

Switch to the Wind setting and drag from the base of a plant or prop to its tip to set up natural, gradual sway. The base stays still while the tip sways the most.

  • Live Preview: Turn on "Simulate Wind" to watch the sway play out in real time, right in the preview window, before you save.
  • No Extra Cost: This movement is stored directly on the model and costs nothing extra to run in your game.

Saving Colors Directly on the Model: What to Expect

Saving your colors directly on the model (rather than as an image) is the recommended option, but it works a little differently from a normal image and has a few trade-offs worth knowing about, understanding these will save you time if a result ever looks "off."

Upside: No Extra Image Memory

Every color variation shares the same lightweight setup, so there's no added memory cost, even across thousands of differently-colored copies.

Trade-off: Detail Is Limited by the Model's Shape

An image can hold as much fine detail as you like. Colors stored directly on the model, though, can only be as detailed as the model itself, color can only change at a point where the model actually has a corner. If you need finer detail, add more geometry there (see "Add Detail Automatically" above).

Trade-off: Zones & Glow Need the Other Save Option

Two features, numbered "zones" and glow effects, only work when using the VCCore material in either mode.

Trade-off: Gradients Only Work This Way

Smooth gradients aren't available if you're saving as an image. With the image-shrinking option turned on, there simply isn't enough room in the tiny resulting image to fit a gradient. With it off, different models' images aren't laid out consistently enough to reliably place a gradient on them.

A note on shadowing in crevices: Natural shading in corners and crevices (sometimes called "ambient occlusion") doesn't transfer well from an image onto a model directly, an image usually holds far more detail than the model has corners to represent it. Because of this, when you scan colors from an image but save directly onto the model, this shading starts out blank (no extra shadowing) rather than guessing at something inaccurate. You can paint it back in by hand afterward, or use the automatic shading tool below to calculate a genuine result.
Painted shading can affect lighting, not just looks: If your material has the "Stylized Lighting" option turned on, any shading you paint or calculate isn't purely decorative, it actually factors into how shadows appear. Painting it too dark can end up looking like an unwanted shadow rather than subtle shading.

The 3D Preview Window

Instead of using Unity's normal scene view, the tool opens its own dedicated 3D preview window. This lets you paint a model without needing to place it in your actual game scene first.

Before / After

The window shows your edited version by default. Hold O to briefly peek at the original, unedited version, let go to go back to your edit.

Compare in Your Scene

Press B to temporarily place preview copies directly into your actual game scene, with real lighting, so you can compare them side by side. They clean themselves up automatically when you close the tool.

Auto-Rotate

Press T (or Space) to slowly spin the model, so you can check how it looks from every angle.

Realistic Lighting

The preview reflects your project's actual lighting setup in real time, so metal and shine settings look accurate.

Hide the Preview

Press V to hide the 3D window entirely and free up space for your list of color groups.

Match Your Camera

Two toggle buttons let you line up your Scene or Game view camera with the tool's preview camera.

Box Select

Hold Shift and drag a box around anything in the preview window to select everything inside it at once, instead of clicking each piece one by one.

Live Wind Preview

Turn on "Simulate Wind" while viewing the Wind setting to watch plants and props sway in real time, right in the preview window.

Toolbar & Shortcut Reference

Once you've scanned a model, the toolbar underneath the preview window gives you these tools. Most shortcuts are turned off while you're typing in a text box (like the save-path field).

If you do something else in your scene right after painting, that gets undone first, and your paint stroke comes up next.

Keeping Patterns Consistent & Smoothing Edges

Found in the color-editing panel, these settings help patterns look consistent in size and let you control how rounded or sharp your model's edges appear:

Keeping Pattern Size Consistent

Helps patterns like wood grain or fabric weave look the same size across different parts of your model, instead of stretched in some spots and squished in others. Two buttons work this out automatically for you.

Rounding Edges & Angle Control

Turning on edge-rounding for a selected piece reveals an angle slider (0°–180°) that controls how it looks:

  • 0° - Keeps the original blocky, flat-faceted look completely untouched.
  • 10°–60° - Gently smooths small creases while keeping sharp corners sharp.
  • 120°–180° - Rounds off even steep angles into soft, smooth curves.
Works at any size: Edge-rounding and gradients are calculated accurately whether your model is a giant dragon, a small dog, or a tiny campfire, regardless of how it was scaled when imported.

One-Click Material Looks (Feature IDs)

When saving directly on the model, you can give any painted piece a realistic surface look with one click, instead of having to hand-paint the detail yourself.

Select a piece, then use the dropdown menu to choose from these looks:

  • Default (None) - Just your painted color and settings, with no extra surface detail added.
  • Wood - Adds both a wood-tone color tint and a wood-grain surface pattern, the only option here that changes the color as well as the surface detail.
  • Bark, Steel, Copper, Cast Iron, Leather, Cloth, Fur, Stone, Rope, Foliage - Each adds its own realistic surface pattern (like scratches, weave, or grain) without changing the color underneath.

Fine-Tuning a Material Look

Once you've picked a look, a few more controls appear:

  • Pattern Size: Makes the pattern (like wood grain) bigger or smaller.
  • Pattern Rotation: Rotates the pattern, useful if it's running in the wrong direction, like sideways wood grain.
  • Smooth the Pattern's Edges: Blends the pattern smoothly across the model's surface so it doesn't look tiled or repetitive, with separate strength sliders for regular edges and for sharper seams.
  • Add Wind to the Pattern: Adds a swaying motion to the surface detail, with a strength slider (or use the Gradient tool's Wind setting for more natural, directional sway).
One-time setup may be needed: If your material hasn't been set up to support these looks yet, choosing one will show a warning with a button that turns the right setting on for you automatically.

Zones (Tagging Areas for Later Use)

When saving directly on the model, you can also tag each piece with a "zone" number from 0 to 7. This doesn't change how anything looks right now inside the tool, it's a way to mark different areas so your game's code or shader can treat them differently later on (for example, applying random color variety only within certain zones).

  • Switch to the Zones setting to see each piece shown in a distinct zone color instead of its actual paint color.
  • With a piece selected, either drag the Zone slider (0–7), or paint directly in the preview the same way you'd paint a color, while this setting is active, your brush applies a zone number instead of a color.
  • Zone 7 is the default/fallback zone; use 0–6 for zones you plan to use in your own game logic or shader effects.
Using an image instead of on-model colors? This zone-painting feature only works when saving directly on the model. For an image-based model that still needs zones, set up the material a different way, which has a dedicated image slot for zone data instead.

Managing Your Color Palettes

Two side-by-side color grids help you keep your color choices consistent across different models.

  • Your Personal Palette (Left): 18 color slots that you fill in yourself. These are saved on your computer and stick around across every project. Click any slot to load that color into your brush.
  • Colors From This Model (Right): Automatically fills in with whatever colors were found when you scanned the current model.
  • Copy Button: Copies the colors from the current model straight into your Personal Palette, so you can reuse them on other models later.

You can also save or load your Personal Palette as a small image file, useful for backing it up or sharing a consistent color scheme with teammates.

Handling Models With Multiple Materials

If your model uses more than one material, each one that has its own image gets its own section in the settings panel, labeled by name. In each section you can independently set that material's base color, along with its metal, shine, and shadowing look, either as one combined image or as separate ones, depending on how that material was originally set up. Leave anything blank to keep whatever the tool automatically detected.

Automatic Shading Tool

A separate tool (not part of the main painting workflow) that calculates realistic shading in corners and crevices by simulating how light would actually be blocked around your model, rather than relying on any pre-made image.

  • Detail Level: How many light samples are taken at each point, higher gives a cleaner result but takes longer to calculate.
  • Max Distance: How far a simulated ray of light can travel before it's counted as unblocked.
  • Strength: How strong the resulting shading looks overall.
  • Smoothing: Reduces any graininess in the result.
  • Where to Save the Result: Choose whether the shading is stored directly on the model, or saved as a separate image (with a button to automatically match the size of your other images).

Click the button to run it. This tool is hidden if you're saving as an image with the image-shrinking option turned on, the shading calculation needs each part of the model to have its own separate space in the image, and that option specifically removes that separate space to save disk size. Rather than produce a broken result, the tool simply hides itself in that case.

Saving Your Work

Once you're happy with your changes, use the buttons at the bottom of the window to save. Which ones you see depends on what you're working on:

  • Apply to Scene Object: Only shows up if you're editing something already placed in your scene. Saves your new version to disk, keeps a backup copy of the original, and automatically swaps the new version into your scene so you can keep adjusting it.
  • Create New Version: Saves a separate, standalone copy to your chosen folder, without touching the model you started with.

A separate setting controls what happens to the material file itself each time you save:

  • Make a New Copy Each Time: Creates a fresh material file every time you save.
  • Share One Copy: Every save in the same session shares a single material file instead of getting its own.
  • Reuse the Existing Material: Points every save at your original material with no new file created at all.
  • Edit the Original Directly: Changes your existing material file itself, instead of creating anything new.
  • Leave It Alone: Doesn't touch the material assignment at all.

Whichever save button you use, here's what happens behind the scenes:

  1. Makes a copy of your original model.
  2. Applies your painted colors to that copy.
  3. Stores your metal, shine, and shadowing settings efficiently on the model.
  4. Applies any edge-rounding you set up.
  5. Saves the new model (and a new image, if you chose that save option) to your chosen folder.
  6. Creates or updates a ready-to-use version that's already set up with the right material.
Check memory usage before saving: A memory-check panel (and the F shortcut) let you preview how much memory your new version will use before you save, so you can see the savings ahead of time instead of only finding out afterward.

Troubleshooting & Common Questions

I turned on "Split Disconnected Islands" but it still won't let me paint individual pieces separately.
Make sure "Group Similar" (Ctrl+G) is turned off with both on, similarly colored pieces get grouped back together even after being split.
How do I paint wind sway onto plants or cloth?
Switch to the Wind setting, turn on Gradient (G), then click at the base of the plant or stem and drag toward the tip. Turn on "Simulate Wind" in the toolbar to watch it sway live in the preview.
What's the difference between the three selection types?
Single Surface picks one small triangle at a time for precise edits. Connected Region picks a whole patch that shares one continuous area on the model's image. Whole Piece picks an entire separate part (like a hair clump or armor plate) in one click. Cycle between them with the I key.
Selecting a piece isn't highlighting it correctly, even though I can still change its color.
This is usually caused by mirrored parts of the model sharing the same image space. Try creating a new version of the model and using that as your target instead.
Why won't my low-poly rocks or logs round off when I turn on edge-rounding?
Blocky, low-detail shapes have sharp angles between their surfaces (often 60°–90° or more). Raise the edge-rounding angle slider higher, toward 120°–180°, to let those larger angles round off too. A setting of 0° keeps everything completely flat.
Can I round off some parts without rounding the whole model?
Yes, edge-rounding is set individually per piece. You can round off organic parts (like wood, rock, or creature bodies) while leaving hard-surface parts (like metal blades or armor) crisp and flat.
I clicked on the model to paint, but nothing happened.
Check that Eraser mode isn't accidentally turned on. Also make sure you're viewing the right setting for what you're trying to change, for example, you can't paint a metal look while viewing plain color.
I can't find the one-click material look options on my pieces.
Those options only appear when you're saving directly on the model. If you're saving as an image instead, the tool assumes you want a plain picture and hides those options.
Undo undid something else in my scene instead of my last brush stroke.
Undo works through Unity's normal history for your whole project, not a separate list just for this tool. So if you did something else in your scene right after painting, that gets undone first, keep pressing Undo and your paint stroke will come up next.
My saved model shows up bright pink.
This means your project is missing a material the tool needs. Make sure everything from the package was imported properly, and that your project's rendering settings match what the tool requires.
The tool feels slow or laggy while I'm painting a detailed model.
The tool checks your exact click position against every single surface for precision, which takes a little longer on very detailed models. If you're working with a model that has 50,000+ triangles, try increasing the color-grouping tolerance to reduce the number of separate pieces.
