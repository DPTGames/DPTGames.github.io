Player Collision & Surface Effects

Use one player component for reactive grass, biome particles, footstep audio and mathematical shoe or bare-foot footprints.

Add Player Surface Effects (DPT_PlayerSurfaceEffects) to the player. Its Enable Scatter Interaction switch controls nearby scatter bending and movement-triggered scatter particles/audio. Enable Surface Effects controls particles, footsteps and footprints from the terrain beneath the player. You can use either part independently.

Author vegetation and ambient effects in the 3D Scatter Guide. Use this page for player setup, contact detection, biome rules and footstep timing.

Built-in particles and footprint silhouettes are generated procedurally. Footprints need no decals, textures or authored mesh assets. Audio needs actual Audio Clips; Custom particles need your own ParticleSystem prefab.

Existing projects: the former DPT_ScatterInteractor is retained for compatibility. Loaded scene components are consolidated into Player Surface Effects with their interaction settings. Use the combined component for new setups; a second interactor is not required.

Visual interaction does not add collision. Grass bending and footprints do not create colliders, deform the terrain or provide a player controller.

Make grass react to a player

  1. Open 3D Scatter in the Vertex 3D Terrain window. Expand your grass Scatter Object and choose Meshes or Meshes + Particles in Scatter Output, below Target Biomes.
  2. Under Placement, enable Player Interaction and set Bend Distance above zero. Start around 0.35 metres for ordinary grass.
  3. Use a compatible material, starting with the supplied SH_DPT_VCTerrain shader.
  4. Add Player Surface Effects to the player. Enable Enable Scatter Interaction.
  5. Assign a foot-level marker to Interaction Origin, or use Interaction Offset to move the influence centre near the grass roots.
  6. Start with Interaction Radius 1, Interaction Height 0.75 and Interaction Strength 1. Move through grass in Play mode.
  7. For movement particles or rustling, use Player Movement in the scatter effect/audio controls. These triggers do not require Player Interaction or a nonzero Bend Distance; those mesh controls enable visible bending.

Scatter bending can also be inspected while editing when the Preview button beside the 3D Scatter header is enabled. Player surface particles, footsteps, footprints and scatter audio play in Play mode.

You can switch Enable Surface Effects off when the component is only needed for scatter interaction. Keep the component itself enabled.

A walking character: use a foot marker as Interaction Origin, Interaction Radius 0.8, Interaction Height 0.5 and Interaction Strength 1. Set the grass Bend Distance to 0.35. Wind remains an independent ambient movement control.

Particles and sounds from disturbed plants

Open the grass Scatter Object, choose Meshes + Particles, then expand Particle Effect 1. Select Grass Flecks or Pollen and set Emission Trigger → Player Movement. The player needs Player Surface Effects with Enable Scatter Interaction on. Visible grass bending is optional.

Use + Add Particle Effect to layer effects on the same placements, for example pollen plus flecks. Each effect has its own preset, trigger, colours, noise quality, emitter fraction, rate, offset and limits. The parent Shared Particle Limit caps the combined living particles. Remove Effect is red.

For rustling, expand Scatter Audio, enable audio, assign clips and choose Player Movement. Tune Minimum Movement Speed and Sound Cooldown. Continuous audio is available for ambient loops. Footstep audio belongs in the player’s biome entries instead.

Use Particle Colors to keep Preset Colors, choose a Single Color or use Random Between Two Colors. Colour alpha multiplies the original opacity; lifetime fading still applies. Changes affect new particles.

Player Movement uses proximity to roots and the swept interaction area. Wind does not trigger it. Editing particle preview is offered for Continuous emission only: enable the main 3D Scatter Preview and the effect’s Preview Particles, and enable Scene view Effects/Particle Systems.

Set up biome surface effects

A surface-effect entry can serve several biomes and combine particles, footprints and audio. These effects follow the sampled terrain beneath the player, independently of scatter placements.

  1. Use Bake Surface Data after authoring. Keep the baked data assigned to DPT_TerrainSurfaceTarget, with a terrain MeshCollider that matches the baked triangle layout. Rebake after geometry, topology or painted biome changes.
  2. On Player Surface Effects, enable Enable Surface Effects. Set Foot Point to a marker near the soles, or use Foot Offset. The offset is relative to Foot Point when assigned.
  3. Expand Surface Detection. Include terrain in Surface Layers and exclude the player. Start with Probe Lift 0.2 and Surface Reach 0.4 metres. Footsteps and footprints also need contact within Foot Contact Distance, default 0.06 metres.
  4. Click Load Scene Terrain Data to add unique baked data from loaded scene Surface Targets. Surface Data Sources supplies named biome choices and a mesh-matched fallback; the hit terrain tile’s own Surface Target takes priority.
  5. Click Add Surface Effect, give the entry a name and select its biomes. Use Add Biome for another target or Add All for every named biome currently available from Surface Data Sources. Add All removes unassigned/duplicate choices; it is a saved selection, not a wildcard for future biomes.
  6. Choose a particle preset, or None for footprints/audio alone. Turn on Footprints and/or Footstep Audio as needed.
  7. Set Minimum Movement Speed and the relevant effect controls, then test while walking in Play mode. Expand Effect Limits and Quality to tune the budgets and Visual Layer.
ControlMeaning / default
Foot Point / Foot OffsetSampling point near the soles. Offset uses the assigned point’s local axes, otherwise the player’s transform.
Surface LayersPhysics layers checked for terrain. Player descendants and trigger colliders are ignored. A nearer ordinary collider blocks terrain behind it.
Probe Lift / Surface ReachRay starts 0.2 metres above the foot point and reaches 0.4 metres below, along the player’s local up direction.
Foot Contact DistanceMaximum foot-to-ground gap for footsteps and footprints; 0.06 metres. Raising particle Surface Offset does not correct a foot point placed above this gap.
Movement Teleport DistanceFoot-point movements larger than 5 metres skip the movement trail. Separate from Scatter Teleport Distance.
Biomes / Add AllSeveral biome IDs can share one entry. Unassigned is not an all-biomes option. Load data before using Add All.
Particle PresetBuilt-in preset, Custom with a ParticleSystem prefab, or None. The first newly added entry defaults to Grass Flecks.
Minimum Movement SpeedPer-entry movement threshold; default 0.1 metres/second. Applies to movement particles and automatic footprint/audio steps.
Emissions Per MetreParticle emission events per metre travelled; default 8. Particle emission remains movement-based in Animation Events footstep mode.
Surface Offset / ScaleParticle spawn offset along the hit normal and scale; defaults 0.03 metres / 1. Footprints have their own offset and size.
Particle ColorsPreset Colors, Single Color or Random Between Two Colors. Available when the entry has particle output.

The first enabled matching entry is selected independently for particles, footprints and audio. A footprint-only entry does not block particles or audio in a later matching entry. Keep related effects in one entry when they share biome targets; order overlapping entries deliberately.

New entries after the first copy the previous entry’s settings, including footprint shape, size, colour, lifetime/fade, movement speed and audio. Their biome list resets to an unassigned choice. Settings are copied independently, so changing the new entry does not change the previous one. Use the red Remove Surface Effect button to delete an entry.

Runtime biome queries resolve the hit tile’s current live biome and sufficiently strong dusting. Add the dusting biome to your targets if snow or ice should change the response. Existing particles finish their lifetimes when emission stops; footprints keep their own lifetime. The component does not calculate water depth, buoyancy or collision force.

Mathematical footprints

  1. In a surface-effect entry, select the biome targets and choose Particle Preset → None if particles are unnecessary.
  2. Enable the separate Footprints switch, then choose Footprint Shape → Shoe or Bare Foot.
  3. Set Footprint Size, Colour, Lifetime, Fade and Offset. Walk in Play mode with the Foot Point near the ground.
Footprint controlMeaning / default
Footprint ShapeShoe has a smooth, solid sole with rounded toe/heel, a shallow inside arch and mirrored left/right placement. Bare Foot has a procedural sole and toes.
Footprint SizeWidth and length in metres; new default 0.12 × 0.32. Previously saved sizes stay as configured.
Footprint ColourPer-entry colour and opacity; default dark brown with alpha 0.6.
Footprint Lifetime / Footprint Fade20-second total lifetime, with a 5-second fade at the end. Fade is part of the lifetime, not extra time afterward.
Footprint OffsetSmall distance along the hit surface normal to reduce flickering; default 0.003 metres. Excessive offsets can appear to float.
Footprint Limit / Footprint DistanceUnder Effect Limits and Quality: defaults 128 marks and 60 metres. The oldest marks are evicted at the limit. Offscreen/out-of-range marks are hidden while their lifetimes continue.
Visual LayerRendering layer for footprints and particles, independent of the player’s collision layer; default layer 0. Include it in the game camera’s Culling Mask.

Footprints use a generated quad and a mathematical silhouette in the existing procedural particle shader. They do not need a decal, imported texture or shoe model. They sit on the sampled surface and receive ambient light and the main light’s realtime shadows in URP. They do not carve or indent the ground.

Keep lifetime, fade, size, offset and colour per surface-effect entry: snow can disappear at a different rate from a road. Use several biomes in one entry when they share the same footprint. Adding another entry copies the previous settings so you only need to change the differences.

Snow and roads: make a Snow Steps entry targeting all snow/ice biomes, with None particles or Snow Puffs and a short footprint lifetime. Add a Road Steps entry, select the road biomes and change its footprint colour and lifetime. The shared component timing and limits still apply to both.

Footstep timing and audio

Particles follow distance travelled. Footstep Timing controls the alternating footprint and footstep-audio contacts together. Configure it once in the component’s Footsteps and Footprints section.

ControlMeaning / default
Distance TravelledAutomatic alternating steps while moving with surface contact. Step Distance defaults to 0.6 metres. Tune to match the character’s stride.
Animation EventsAutomatic distance steps are disabled. Call EmitFootstep() at each animated foot contact. It alternates the left/right placement. Place the component on the Animator’s object or forward the animation event from your animation script.
Foot SpacingDistance from the centre sample to each foot across the hit surface; default 0.09 metres.
Footstep AudioEnable inside a surface-effect entry and assign Audio Clips. One non-null clip is chosen randomly per new sound. Choose None particles and disable Footprints for audio-only steps.
Volume / Pitch RangeSpatial audio volume and randomized pitch; defaults 0.5 and 0.95–1.05.
Near Distance / Audio DistanceFull-volume distance and maximum audible distance; defaults 1 / 25 metres with linear spatial attenuation.
Footstep Sound LimitMaximum simultaneous voices for this player, default 8, adjustable from 1 to 32. New sounds are skipped if all voices are busy.

Use an enabled Audio Listener on the game camera and test in Play mode. Clips are required; the package does not synthesize footstep recordings. Footsteps require an eligible biome and a Foot Point within Foot Contact Distance.

Animation events explicitly request a step and still check terrain contact and biome settings. The automatic movement threshold does not block the explicit event. There is no separate impact/landing sound component or impact-speed configuration in this workflow.

Materials and custom meshes

The material must support DPT scatter interaction. GPU Instancing alone is not enough. Start with the supplied SH_DPT_VCTerrain shader and verify the response before replacing it with a custom material.

Bending uses the mesh’s local Y height within its bounds: the lowest part stays anchored and displacement increases toward the tip. For custom grass, author its root-to-tip direction along local +Y and check its pivot and bounds. A sideways mesh or unrelated geometry included in the same bounds can produce an unexpected bend mask.

A custom shader needs the DPT scatter vertex interaction path and its support property as well as the normal scatter instancing setup. Adding only the wind function does not automatically add player bending. Keep the vertex path consistent in the visible and shadow passes.

Use Terrain Material Settings for wind tuning and the custom mesh setup for variants, source meshes and materials.

Players, NPCs and limits

Add Player Surface Effects with Enable Scatter Interaction to each nearby actor that should influence scatter. Player and NPC components use the same shared shader data; no trigger collider or collision callback is required for the visual bend.

The system supplies up to eight enabled, positive-strength interactors at once. It uses registration order rather than automatically selecting the eight closest actors. Enable only the actors that need to contribute when a scene has a large crowd.

When several actors overlap, their pushes combine while respecting the scatter group’s Bend Distance. Height Tolerance helps keep responses separate on bridges, cave floors and stacked spaces.

Recovery follows the actor’s current position. Grass bending does not retain a bent trail or simulate spring recovery after the actor leaves. The separate Footprints feature can leave timed marks on terrain.

Physical collision and gameplay

Keep the terrain’s collision mesh and your player’s movement/collision setup separate from Player Surface Effects. The interactor supplies a rendering influence; it is not a player controller and does not make the character stand on the terrain.

Instanced scatter does not generate an individual collider for each rock, tree or prop. For an obstacle that must block the player or react physically, use a separate scene object with the appropriate collider and gameplay behaviour. The visual grass can remain instanced around it.

Flow, Lake ripples, dusting and scatter bending change appearance. They do not add water buoyancy, icy friction, slippery collision or a moving water volume. Connect gameplay behaviour through your own controller or the Demo & Runtime Guide. Baked surface queries do not measure a bent grass vertex or a particle.

Performance and limits

Scatter interaction adds work to the compatible vertex shader without rebuilding placements or creating a GameObject per grass instance. Visible vertex count and active actors affect cost. Scatter culling bounds include the configured Bend Distance; wind bounds remain separate.

Player particles, footsteps and footprints share surface detection and biome rule selection. Particle systems and audio sources are pooled. Footprint rendering uses instanced batches, precomputed bounds, camera frustum/distance culling, cached draw data when the view and marks are unchanged, and GPU lifetime fading.

Limits cap the budget rather than increasing emission. Profile with your game camera, target resolution and target device. Culling and pooling reduce unnecessary work but do not guarantee a particular frame rate.

Support

If you need help, include your Unity version, screenshots of the Scatter Object and Player Surface Effects, the mesh and material used, and whether the issue occurs in Scene view or Play mode. For height problems, show the player centre relative to the grass roots.
