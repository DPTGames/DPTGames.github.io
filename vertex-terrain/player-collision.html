Player Collision & Scatter Interaction

Make grass bend and release flecks or pollen as players and NPCs pass through it, then add splashes, dust or snow effects for the terrain beneath their feet.

The Scatter Interactor supplies a position and an area of influence to compatible scatter shaders. Nearby grass tips move away from the interactor, with a smooth falloff toward the edge of its radius. The roots stay anchored and the grass returns toward its resting shape as the player moves away.

Configure the grass in the 3D Scatter Guide, then add the player response described here. Wind, live biome animation and particle emission remain separate controls.

Choose the preset for the appearance and the trigger or component for when it emits. The same preset list is available in all three particle inspectors.

Grass bending is a visual response. It changes rendered scatter vertices while keeping placement data and GPU instancing intact. It does not create grass colliders, push a Rigidbody or change the terrain’s collision mesh. For physical terrain or obstacle collision, see Physical collision and gameplay.

Make grass react to a player

Where is Scatter Output? In the Vertex 3D Terrain window, expand 3D Scatter, then click your grass scatter object’s name or arrow to open its controls. The Scatter Output dropdown is directly below Target Biomes and above the Placement heading.

Choose Meshes for grass meshes, or Both for grass meshes with a particle effect.

  1. In the Vertex 3D Terrain window, expand 3D Scatter. Create or open your grass Scatter Object and confirm that it already appears on the intended Terrain Atlas biome.
  2. At the top of that object’s controls, find Scatter Output below Target Biomes and above Placement. Choose Meshes or Both.
  3. Under Placement, enable Player Interaction, beside Static Scatter. Bend Distance then appears below the buttons; start around 0.3–0.5 metres.
  4. Use a compatible material, starting with the supplied SH_DPT_VCTerrain shader.
  5. On the player or a child near its feet, use Add Component and search for DPT_ScatterInteractor / Scatter Interactor.
  6. Set its centre near the grass roots, using Centre Offset if the player’s transform is higher up.
  7. Start with Radius 1, Height Tolerance 0.75 and Strength 1. Select the component to inspect its gizmo.
  8. Move the player through the grass in Play mode. Adjust the radius for the footprint and Bend Distance for how far the tips move.
  9. Enable wind separately if you also want ambient movement.

A walking character: place the interactor on a child at foot level. Use Radius 0.8, Height Tolerance 0.5, Strength 1 and grass Bend Distance 0.35. Increase radius if the response begins too close to the character; reduce bend distance if short grass folds too far.

The component also runs while editing. With Scene Preview enabled, moving the interactor transform lets you inspect the grass response before entering Play mode.

Scatter Interactor controls

ControlMeaningHow to tune it
Centre OffsetOffset from the transform in its local axes. Transform rotation and scale affect the resulting centre.Put the centre near the player’s feet rather than its chest. A foot-level child can use an offset of zero.
RadiusInteraction reach in world metres, measured in each scatter instance’s surface plane.Default 1. Increase it for a wider response around the actor.
Height ToleranceAllowed separation above or below the grass root, measured along the instance’s local up direction.Default 0.75. Keep it small enough that players on another floor do not affect grass beneath them.
Strength: 0–1How strongly this actor pushes the nearby grass.Default 1. Reduce it for a gentler response; 0 removes this actor’s influence.
Maximum Movement StepMaximum centre movement per sample that can trigger scatter particles.Default 5 metres. Larger jumps are treated as teleports and do not emit a swept trail. This does not disable visual bending at the new position.

Radius controls where the effect is felt; it does not set the maximum displacement. The scatter group’s Bend Distance controls how far tips may move. Both distance and height influence fade smoothly toward their limits.

The selected-component gizmo shows the centre, radial reach and height band. On slopes or curved terrain, interaction uses each scatter instance’s surface plane; the gizmo is a setup aid rather than a drawn copy of every instance’s influence calculation.

Particle emission anchors remain at the placement transforms; shader bending does not move them along with the grass tip. Use the particle Emission Offset to position an effect around the plant’s resting shape.

Particles from disturbed grass

Find the particle controls: Vertex 3D Terrain window → 3D Scatter → open your grass Scatter Object → set Scatter Output to Both → expand Particle Effect. The Particle Preset and Emission Trigger dropdowns are at the top of Particle Effect.

Preview Particles is a button: blue means enabled. It controls editing preview; runtime emission uses the chosen trigger.

To flick grass flecks or pollen into the air as a player runs through a field, use Both on the existing grass Scatter Object, enable Player Interaction and set Bend Distance above zero. Inside Particle Effect, select Grass Flecks or Pollen from the Particle Preset dropdown, then choose Player Movement for Emission Trigger.

The same Scatter Interactor supplies the moving contact area. Emitter Fraction controls how many grass placements can release particles; Rate / Emitter sets their frequency while influenced. Minimum Movement Speed prevents stationary emission and small movement jitter. Emission Offset positions particles relative to the resting placement. Wind does not trigger this mode.

Particle-only groups can also react inside the moving interactor’s area. The test uses root proximity and height rather than measuring the displacement of each rendered vertex. See the scatter reaction setup and available presets.

Splashes, dust and surface effects

DPT_PlayerSurfaceEffects releases particles as the player moves over selected terrain biomes. It works independently of scatter placements, so water splashes and rock dust do not need a grass or mesh scatter group.

  1. Use Bake Surface Data in the terrain window after authoring the terrain. The bake adds or updates DPT_TerrainSurfaceTarget and its data asset. Ensure the terrain has a MeshCollider matching the baked triangle layout. Rebuild and rebake after geometry or painted biome changes.
  2. On the player, use Add Component and search for DPT_PlayerSurfaceEffects. Set Foot Offset so the point is near the feet. No Scatter Interactor is required for this component.
  3. Set Surface Layers to the terrain layers and exclude the player’s layer. Start with Probe Lift 0.2 and Surface Reach 0.4 metres.
  4. Optionally assign a baked data asset to Biome Names From to show named biome choices in the inspector. Runtime queries use the data belonging to the terrain actually hit.
  5. Click Add Surface Effect. Choose the biome explicitly, then choose its Particle Preset: for example Water Splash for water, Rock Dust for rock, Sand Dust for dry ground or Snow Puffs for snow. Use Custom for your own prefab.
  6. Set Emissions Per Metre, Minimum Movement Speed, Surface Offset and Scale for each effect. Start with 8 emissions per metre and Minimum Movement Speed 0.1.
  7. Test in Play mode. Tune Particle Distance and Particle Limit for the game camera.

The bake is useful for these reactions. Surface lookup uses the compact baked triangle data and can work without mesh Read/Write. It resolves the current live biome and sufficiently strong dusting, so a water biome covered by ice can select an ice or snow effect. The effect rules do not consume dynamic biome slots.

Effects follow distance travelled, rather than animation footstep events or measured collision force. They do not calculate water depth or buoyancy. Presets select appearance; you decide which biome uses each one.

Standing still, leaving Surface Reach, exceeding Particle Distance or moving onto an unconfigured surface stops new emission. Existing particles finish naturally. A closer ordinary collider blocks the terrain beneath it, preventing terrain dust while standing on another object. This component runs in Play mode; scatter particles have a separate Preview Particles highlight button for editing.

Materials and custom meshes

The material must support DPT scatter interaction. GPU Instancing alone is not enough. Start with the supplied SH_DPT_VCTerrain shader and verify the response before replacing it with a custom material.

Bending uses the mesh’s local Y height within its bounds: the lowest part stays anchored and displacement increases toward the tip. For custom grass, author its root-to-tip direction along local +Y and check its pivot and bounds. A sideways mesh or unrelated geometry included in the same bounds can produce an unexpected bend mask.

A custom shader needs the DPT scatter vertex interaction path and its support property as well as the normal scatter instancing setup. Adding only the wind function does not automatically add player bending. Keep the vertex path consistent in the visible and shadow passes.

Use Terrain Material Settings for wind tuning and the custom mesh setup for variants, source meshes and materials.

Players, NPCs and limits

Add an interactor to each nearby actor that should influence scatter. Player and NPC components use the same shared shader data; no trigger collider or collision callback is required for the visual bend.

The system supplies up to eight enabled, positive-strength interactors at once. It uses registration order rather than automatically selecting the eight closest actors. Enable only the actors that need to contribute when a scene has a large crowd.

When several actors overlap, their pushes combine while respecting the scatter group’s Bend Distance. Height Tolerance helps keep responses separate on bridges, cave floors and stacked spaces.

Recovery follows the actor’s current position. This version does not retain a footprint trail or simulate a spring recovery delay after the actor leaves.

Physical collision and gameplay

Keep the terrain’s collision mesh and your player’s movement/collision setup separate from the Scatter Interactor. The interactor supplies a rendering influence; it is not a player controller and does not make the character stand on the terrain.

Instanced scatter does not generate an individual collider for each rock, tree or prop. For an obstacle that must block the player or react physically, use a separate scene object with the appropriate collider and gameplay behaviour. The visual grass can remain instanced around it.

Flow, Lake ripples, dusting and scatter bending change appearance. They do not add water buoyancy, icy friction, slippery collision or a moving water volume. Connect gameplay behaviour through your own controller or the Demo & Runtime Guide. Baked surface queries do not measure a bent grass vertex or a particle.

Performance and culling

Interaction adds work to the compatible scatter vertex shader without rebuilding placements or creating a GameObject per grass instance. Cost depends on mesh vertex count, visible population and the number of active interactors. Check dense grass and the target device with your game camera.

The renderer accounts for the configured interaction bend distance in its CPU/GPU culling bounds. Use distance, LOD and population limits to keep distant detail manageable. Wind bounds remain a separate setting for wind motion.

Support

If you need help, include your Unity version, screenshots of the Scatter Object and Scatter Interactor, the mesh and material used, and whether the issue occurs in Scene view or Play mode. For height problems, show the player centre relative to the grass roots.
