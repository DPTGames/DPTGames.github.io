Projectiles & Bullet Impacts

Projectile-prefab configuration · Hitscan, swept movement, penetration, ricochets and surface effects

One bullet prefab controls both firing modes

DPT_Firearm supplies the muzzle origin, trigger behaviour and spread. The assigned DPT_FirearmProjectile prefab owns Shot Type, Muzzle Velocity, Maximum Range, Damage, Collision Layers and all impact settings. Hitscan and swept shots share a single impact handler. Add the independent optional DPT_FirearmSurfaceImpact component to configure multiple contact responses by physics layer.

Projectile Prefab Setup

  1. Create a projectile prefab with DPT_FirearmProjectile and a root Rigidbody. Flight uses swept raycasts rather than Rigidbody movement; the script makes the Rigidbody kinematic.
  2. Under the projectile root, add a visible intact bullet child and an optional inactive crushed or deformed bullet child. Assign them to Normal Bullet Visual and Deformed Bullet Visual.
  3. On DPT_Firearm, assign the prefab to Projectile Prefab and set Muzzle Point and Spread Degrees. These are weapon references.
  4. On the projectile prefab, select Shot Type, then configure velocity, range, damage and collision layers. Gravity and drag only affect swept flight.
  5. Optionally assign an Impact Effect Prefab for a separate decal, crater, sparks or dust. Do not use this slot for the crushed bullet child.
  6. For optional material-specific contacts, add DPT_FirearmSurfaceImpact to the same bullet prefab. Add ordered Layer Rules; a matching rule selects Stop, Penetrate, Ricochet or Automatic.
Bullet_Projectile [DPT_FirearmProjectile, Rigidbody]
├── Bullet_Regular       (normal bullet visual)
└── Bullet_Deformed      (inactive until final stop)

Optional component on projectile root: DPT_FirearmSurfaceImpact
Do not create a second crushed-bullet prefab for the impact slot. The deformed mesh is an existing child of the projectile prefab. On impact the script hides the regular child and enables that deformed child.

Hitscan vs Swept Projectile

Mode on projectileBehaviour
HitscanTraces immediately from the firearm muzzle using the projectile range and collision layers. A hit creates an impact instance, then optional penetration and ricochet contacts are resolved in the same shot. A miss has no visual remnant.
Swept ProjectileCreates a visible bullet at the muzzle and advances it in FixedUpdate using swept raycasts. It can continue through matching thin colliders or change direction after a ricochet, with reduced speed. Gravity and drag apply during flight.

Where to change modes: edit Shot Type on the DPT_FirearmProjectile prefab. There is no longer a Shot Type selector on the firearm.

Material-Specific Surface Reactions

Add DPT_FirearmSurfaceImpact to the bullet prefab root alongside DPT_FirearmProjectile. This module is self-contained within DPT.Firearms.Runtime and does not reference the Siege Weapons system or DPT Core runtime. It is optional: a bullet without this module, or without a matching rule, stops at the first contact.

  1. Open the bullet prefab, add DPT_FirearmSurfaceImpact and expand Layer Rules.
  2. Add rules for your physics layers in priority order, with more specific layer masks above broad catch-all masks. The first matching rule wins.
  3. Choose Reaction: Stop, Penetrate, Ricochet or Automatic. Optionally limit a rule to Horizontal Only or Vertical Only using the surface normal relative to world up.
  4. Choose the optional mark, spalling VFX and audio overrides for the layer. Existing Impact Effect Prefab and Impact Sounds on the projectile remain the fallback.
  5. Test both Hitscan and Swept Projectile shots against colliders of known thickness and layer assignment.

Example starting rules: For thin wood, use Penetrate, Maximum Penetration Thickness around 0.16 m and Penetration Velocity Retention 0.65. For metal, try Ricochet with an angle of 65° from the normal and velocity retention 0.5. These are tuning examples, not physically calibrated material values.
Automatic: Ricochet is evaluated at the specified grazing angle and minimum speed. Otherwise penetration is attempted when its speed and thickness tests pass; if neither is valid, the projectile stops. Explicit Penetrate and Ricochet rules likewise stop when their required conditions are not met.

Impact Flow

Hitscan: Immediate raycast → Contact response → Optional ricochet / penetration continuation → Final stop or end of trace.

Swept Projectile: FixedUpdate raycast → Contact response → Continue travelling with retained speed or stop and show deformed bullet.

  • When a bullet stops, its root is positioned using Embed Depth and parented to the struck collider while preserving its world-space pose. Successful penetration and ricochet contacts do not embed the projectile.
  • A valid contact delivers damage through IDPT_FirearmDamageable, scaled by retained speed. Additional penetration or ricochet contacts can deliver further hits.
  • The original bullet child is disabled and the deformed child is enabled when configured. The stopped projectile is removed after Lifetime After Impact.
  • A contact spawns its surface mark, optional spalling VFX and impact sound using the matching surface rule or the projectile fallbacks. Marks are parented to the struck collider.
  • Impacted(Vector3) fires on each contact and can trigger projectile impact shake. The onImpact UnityEvent fires only when the bullet finally stops.
Parenting follows a moving collider but cannot automatically transfer an impact to replacement meshes or newly created destruction fragments. Integrate with your destruction system if that behaviour is required.

Contacts, Final Stops and Event Timing

DPT_FirearmProjectile.Impacted(Vector3) fires for every processed contact, including a ricochet or penetration entry, and therefore also drives optional projectile impact screen shake at those points. The onImpact UnityEvent fires once when the projectile finally comes to rest and its deformed bullet visual is activated. No deformed bullet is left behind during a successful ricochet or penetration.

When penetration or ricochet reduces the projectile speed, later contacts receive reduced damage and physics impulse. Hitscan calculates the continuation immediately rather than spawning a moving bullet, while swept shots continue across physics steps.

Collision and geometry note: Penetration requires a detectable exit surface from the same Collider. Thin closed objects are suitable test cases; an open or single-sided mesh may not expose a usable exit. The surface module does not cut or deform target meshes.

Aiming and Ballistics

  • Shots originate at Muzzle Point using its +Z forward axis. A camera-centred crosshair is not, by itself, a ballistic zero.
  • Set Spread Degrees = 0 on the firearm while calibrating iron sights. Configure the projectile's Shot Type = Hitscan for a straight-line test.
  • Then select Swept Projectile on the bullet prefab and evaluate gravity, muzzle velocity and drop. The demonstration does not implement a dedicated sight zero-distance solver.

Performance Notes

  • Swept bullets use physics raycasts during simulation. Hitscan may perform multiple raycasts in one shot when penetration or ricochet rules allow continuation. The reused raycast buffer avoids allocations for most individual queries, while spawned bullets and effects still allocate.
  • The raycast buffers are reused, but spawning projectile instances and surface effects still allocates. A pooling system is an optional later optimisation.
  • Rigidbody is required on the projectile prefab, but flight is script-controlled and the body is kinematic.
  • Avoid applying duplicate impulses from both Physics Impact Impulse and your target's damage receiver.
  • Use Max Penetrations, Max Ricochets and Maximum Contacts Per Step to bound work per bullet. Large numbers of contact effects, sounds and embedded marks can still affect performance.
